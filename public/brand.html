ELEVEN11 · BRAND03 — THE GRID
The cell

One cell is half an icon square. A 16:9 page is 32 × 18 cells; the form, the masses and the icon all sit on it.

The mass

Every page is a mass drawn in whole cells. Two sides break — top and bottom, one cell in or out. The sides stay straight. No straight cut between pages, ever.

Eleven loose pieces

Each mass sheds exactly 11 pieces: pixels on the ground, holes in the mass.

The calm core

Text and pictures live in the core. The grid never breaks there.

One loud block

One orange thing per page: the colon, or whatever is live.

Layers

The ground grid moves slower than you. The edges break into pixels as they leave the centre of the screen, and the loose pieces fly faster. That is the parallax.

ELEVEN11 · BRAND08 — ELEVEN CUTS · RULES
Whole cells

Every picture is a window cut to whole cells. No rotation, no feathered edge, no blend mode, no tilt.

Three materials

Picture — low-key, teal into orange. Flat — bone or charcoal. Pixel — the picture sampled to the cell, or half of it. Nothing else.

Eleven cuts

A sequence is 11 hard cuts at 12 fps, about 1.4 seconds. No dissolves, no easing. Cut 11 is the landing: the logo or the line.

The calm core

The landing sits on the page itself — charcoal and the grid — with a broken top and bottom. Once it lands, nothing crosses it.

One loud block

Orange once per frame, at most: a block, the flash on cut 10, then the colon or the cursor.

Hold and breathe

While it holds, one window re-cuts on every blink of the colon. A still — a post, a slide, a background — is one frozen cut.

Read before you cut

Every picture gets a meaning map: the subject, up to three meaning points in order, and where its texture is.

Hero

The whole subject. When the logo lands, the subject moves to where the core is not — the logo never covers the meaning.

Detail

One meaning point. The window takes its shape — wide for eyes, tall for a face — and holds the whole point. Never cut through it.

Pixel

Made from texture: the light, the dark, the grain. Never from the meaning itself.
