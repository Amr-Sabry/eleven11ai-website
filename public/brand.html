ELEVEN11 · BRAND03 — THE FIFTH ELEMENT · THE BLOCK · LAW

Numbers 1–11 are the loose pieces. Dashed: the calm core, where the logo and the words live.

01 · Pixel

Every format is cut into square pixels. The short side is always 22 of them: 2 × 11.

02 · Grid

A thin line every pixel, a stronger one every 11. One logo square = 4 pixels, so the logo sits on the grid too.

03 · Block

One rectangle on the grid, or two stepped like the logo. It carries the logo and the words.

04 · Break

One or two sides break, never all four. Whole pixels only: up to 3 out, 1 in, and a few holes.

05 · Eleven

Exactly 11 loose pieces fly off the broken sides: 1 to 3 pixels each, never touching, within 7 pixels.

06 · Calm core

Logo and words stay 3 pixels inside a broken edge and 1 inside a clean one.

07 · Colour

One loud block (orange) per layout, at most one quiet block (charcoal or bone). Behind it: charcoal and the grid.

ELEVEN11 · BRAND09 — THE SCREEN
LIVE · THE LED SITS OUTSIDE THE GLASS
Where the minute lives

11:11 is something you read on a screen. For most of us the first screen was a phone’s: grey-green glass, square pixels, one colour.

The grid, for real

On an LCD the grid is not a guide — it is the glass. Every pixel shows, even when it is off. The 22-pixel law becomes a thing you can touch.

Depth, for free

The crystal floats over the back plate, so every lit pixel casts its shadow one pixel down and right. The same depth our layers move on.

Eleven dead pixels

Every old screen has a few stuck pixels. Ours has exactly eleven — the loose pieces of the block, in shadow grey.

One colour, one LED

The glass has no colour: on, shadow, off, bezel. Orange stays outside it, as the LED — the one thing that is live.

ELEVEN11 · BRAND13 — 11 CUTS · RULES
Whole cells

Every picture is a window cut to whole pixels of the grid. No rotation, no feathered edge, no blend mode, no tilt.

Three materials

Picture — low-key, teal into orange. Flat — bone or charcoal. Pixel — the picture sampled to the grid pixel, or half of it. Nothing else.

Eleven cuts

A sequence is 11 hard cuts at 12 fps, about 1.4 seconds. No dissolves, no easing. Cut 11 is the landing: the logo or the line.

The calm core

The landing sits on the page itself — charcoal and the grid — with a broken top and bottom. Once it lands, nothing crosses it.

One loud block

Orange once per frame, at most: a block, the flash on cut 10, then the colon or the cursor.

Hold and breathe

While it holds, one window re-cuts on every blink of the colon. A still — a post, a slide, a background — is one frozen cut.
